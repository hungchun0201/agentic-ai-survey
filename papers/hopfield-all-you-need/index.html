Hopfield Networks is All You Need

A continuous-state modern Hopfield network whose one-step retrieval rule turns out to be exactly transformer attention — recasting the attention layer as a single associative-memory lookup.

arXiv:2008.02217 ICLR 2021 Associative Memory Attention Mechanism Has Repo

Hubert Ramsauer, Bernhard Schäfl, Johannes Lehner, Philipp Seidl, Michael Widrich, Lukas Gruber, Markus Holzleitner, Milena Pavlović, Geir Kjetil Sandve, Victor Greiff, David Kreil, Michael Kopp, Günter Klambauer, Johannes Brandstetter, Sepp Hochreiter

ELLIS Unit Linz & LIT AI Lab, Johannes Kepler University Linz · IARAI · University of Oslo

1. Why Attention Needed an Explanation

By 2020 the transformer had become the dominant architecture in natural language processing: BERT and its successors set records on task after task, and every one of those gains flowed through the same primitive — the scaled dot-product attention layer $\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T/\sqrt{d_k})\mathbf{V}$. Yet the community understood attention operationally rather than theoretically.

1.1 A concrete failure: the classical Hopfield network cannot remember

An associative memory is a device that stores a set of patterns and, when presented with a noisy or partial query, returns the stored pattern most similar to it. The classical binary Hopfield network of 1982 is the textbook realization of this idea, and it is also where the idea breaks down at scale.

1.2 The obvious fix, and why it is not enough

A natural response is to reach for the ‘modern’ Hopfield networks — the dense associative memory (DAM) line of work. Krotov and Hopfield raised capacity to a term proportional to $d^{n-1}$; Demircigil and colleagues reached $2^{d/2}$. But every one of these DAM models operates on binary states.

The fundamental obstacle. To serve as a layer in a deep network, an associative memory must be continuous and differentiable and must retrieve in a single update. The design space is therefore ‘keep exponential capacity and one-step retrieval while moving from binary to continuous states.’

1.3 Where this sits relative to prior work

The paper joins two conversations: the memory-augmented-network line (NTM, Memory Networks, End-to-End Memory Networks, transformer) and the associative-memory line (Krotov–Hopfield, Demircigil). What every one left on the table is the identity that a continuous-state modern Hopfield network, retrieving in one step, computes precisely the transformer's attention.

1.4 What the paper delivers

With the pain and the obstacle established, the contributions can be stated as answers rather than as a list of features.

Continuous energy & update rule

A new energy function extends the exponential-interaction memory to continuous patterns and states, with a globally convergent update rule.

Exponential capacity, one-step retrieval

Stores a number of patterns exponential in the dimension, retrieves a well-separated pattern in one update, with exponentially small error.

Attention is a Hopfield retrieval

The update rule with query/key/value projections and $\beta = 1/\sqrt{d_k}$ is exactly transformer attention, yielding a characterization of BERT heads.

Three drop-in Hopfield layers

Hopfield, HopfieldPooling, and HopfieldLayer expose the memory as PyTorch modules replacing attention, pooling, and fully-connected layers.

2. Background: the Storage-Capacity Ladder

The paper's new network is the last rung of a ladder the associative-memory literature climbed over four decades, each rung trading one property for more capacity.

2.1 Classical Hopfield network (quadratic interaction)

The 1982 model uses a quadratic interaction $F(x)=x^2$; its capacity ceiling is roughly $0.138\,d$, beyond which spurious minima dominate. Every later rung keeps the energy-minimization framing and changes only $F$.

2.2 Polynomial dense associative memory

Krotov and Hopfield replaced $F(x)=x^2$ with $F(x)=x^n$, raising capacity to a quantity proportional to $d^{\,n-1}$. The model stays binary, but establishes that the shape of $F$ governs capacity.

2.3 Exponential dense associative memory

Demircigil's exponential interaction $F(x)=\exp(x)$ gives capacity $2^{d/2}$. Capacity is no longer the constraint; what remains binding is that the states are binary. The paper's continuous energy begins from this exponential energy.

3. The Continuous Energy Function and Update Rule

This section defines the new energy, states the update rule that descends it, and records the three theorems — global convergence, one-step retrieval, and exponential capacity — that certify the memory is usable inside a deep network.

3.1 The log-sum-exp building block

With $N$ stored patterns in $\mathbf{X}$, largest norm $M$, and query $\boldsymbol{\xi}$, the exponential interaction is handled through the convex log-sum-exp function:

(1) $$\operatorname{lse}(\beta, \mathbf{z}) = \beta^{-1}\log\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N} \exp(\beta\, z_i)\right)$$

The inverse-temperature $\beta > 0$ controls sharpness: large $\beta$ approaches the maximum, small $\beta$ averages over all patterns. This parameter reappears as the attention scaling $1/\sqrt{d_k}$.

3.2 The new energy function

Two changes turn the binary energy into a continuous one: a logarithm converts the exponential interaction into the $\operatorname{lse}$ term, and a quadratic term $\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\xi}^T\boldsymbol{\xi}$ bounds the otherwise-unbounded norm of a continuous state.

(2) $$\mathrm{E} = -\operatorname{lse}(\beta, \mathbf{X}^T\boldsymbol{\xi}) + \tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\xi}^T\boldsymbol{\xi} + \beta^{-1}\log N + \tfrac{1}{2}M^2$$

The last two terms make the energy non-negative and bounded ($0 \leqslant \mathrm{E} \leqslant 2M^2$). The energy is a concave part ($-\operatorname{lse}$) plus a convex part (the quadratic), a decomposition the convergence proof relies on.

3.3 CCCP: deriving the update rule

Retrieval moves the query downhill on this energy. Writing $\mathbf{p} = \operatorname{softmax}(\beta\, \mathbf{X}^T\boldsymbol{\xi})$ for the query-to-pattern similarities, the update replaces the query with the $\mathbf{p}$-weighted average of the stored patterns:

(3) $$\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\text{new}} = \mathbf{X}\,\operatorname{softmax}(\beta\, \mathbf{X}^T\boldsymbol{\xi})$$

This is the equation the whole paper turns on: on the right, a $\operatorname{softmax}$ of query-pattern dot products; on the left, a weighted sum of patterns. It is visibly the same shape as an attention layer, and Section 4 makes the correspondence exact. The update is derived rather than guessed, and the algorithm that derives it is the Concave–Convex Procedure (CCCP): at the current state it linearizes the concave $-\operatorname{lse}$ term and minimizes the resulting convex surrogate, and Eq. (3) is the closed-form minimizer of that surrogate. Each step therefore cannot increase the energy, so the iteration genuinely descends rather than merely resembling attention. The property CCCP guarantees is convergence to a stationary point of the energy, not necessarily to the specific pattern the query intended to retrieve. Section 6.1 shows the resulting executable iteration and its stopping rule.

3.4 Three theorems that make it a layer

Three appendix-proven results discharge the requirements from Section 1; each certifies one property the memory must have to be a forward-once, gradient-trained layer.

Theorem Statement Why it matters
Global convergence Iterating Eq. (3) drives $\mathrm{E}(\boldsymbol{\xi}^t) \to \mathrm{E}(\boldsymbol{\xi}^*)$ from any start. Retrieval is well-defined; it never diverges.
One-step retrieval After one update, $\lVert f(\boldsymbol{\xi}) - \mathbf{x}_i^* \rVert$ is exponentially small in the separation $\Delta_i$. One forward activation suffices — matches a deep layer.
Exponential capacity Storable random patterns $\geq \sqrt{p}\, c^{(d-1)/4}$; e.g. $c \geq 3.15$ at $d=20$, $c \geq 1.37$ at $d=75$. Capacity survives the move to continuous states.

3.5 Three kinds of fixed point

What the update converges to depends on how well the queried pattern is separated from the rest; the paper identifies three regimes, which later make an attention head interpretable.

(a) Single-pattern fixed point

A well-separated pattern has its own nearby fixed point; the softmax collapses onto it and the query retrieves it exactly.

(b) Metastable state

Several similar patterns share a fixed point that is their average; the query retrieves a summary of the group.

(c) Global fixed point

When no pattern is separated, the softmax is near uniform ($p_i \approx 1/N$) and the query converges to the mean of all patterns.
Figure A.1 (from paper): The three cases as energy landscapes. Left ($\beta=1$): well-separated patterns sit in their own basins, stored individually. Middle ($\beta=1$): three similar patterns share a basin, giving a metastable average $\mathbf{m}_x$. Right ($\beta=0.2$): a low $\beta$ flattens the landscape to one global basin.

4. The Equivalence: Attention is One Hopfield Update

The title is a claim; this section is its proof. Eq. (3) already has the shape of attention; introducing the query/key/value projections and fixing $\beta$ makes the two literally the same formula.

4.1 Adding the projections

A transformer projects its inputs first. Mapping stored patterns via $\mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{W}_K^T\mathbf{y}_i$ and queries via $\boldsymbol{\xi}_i = \mathbf{W}_Q^T\mathbf{r}_i$ gives $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{Y}\mathbf{W}_K$, $\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{R}\mathbf{W}_Q$, $\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{Y}\mathbf{W}_K\mathbf{W}_V$.

(10) $$\mathbf{Z} = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}\, \mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T\right)\mathbf{V}$$

Setting $\beta = 1/\sqrt{d_k}$ makes the projected update rule exactly Eq. (10) — scaled dot-product attention. Self-attention is the case $\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{Y}$. The empirically-chosen temperature $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ is the inverse-temperature of a Hopfield retrieval.

What the equivalence buys. Once attention is a Hopfield retrieval, the three-regime taxonomy applies to every head: a head is characterized by how many patterns its softmax averages over — the size of its metastable state — measurable directly from a trained model.

5. Three Hopfield Layers

Because the network is continuous, differentiable, and retrieves in one update, it drops into a deep architecture as a layer. The paper exposes three, differing in what is query, what is stored memory, and which is learned.

Figure 2 (from paper): Left: a standard deep network propagating a vector or a set of vectors. Right: the same network with layers equipped with associative memories via Hopfield layers, giving each layer access to stored patterns.

5.1 Hopfield — associate two sets

The Hopfield layer directly realizes Eq. (10): it associates state (query) patterns $\mathbf{R}$ with stored (key) patterns $\mathbf{Y}$. With skip connections in a ResNet block, a stack of these is identical to the transformer and BERT.

5.2 HopfieldPooling — summarize a set

The HopfieldPooling layer fixes and learns the query while the memory holds the input set $\mathbf{Y}$. A single learned query returns a softmax-weighted average of the matching patterns, compressing a variable-sized set to a fixed-size representation — realizing multiple-instance learning.

5.3 HopfieldLayer — a learned lookup table

The HopfieldLayer reverses the previous case: the query comes from the input while the stored patterns are fixed and learned. It can behave like an SVM, $k$-NN, or LVQ, and can replace a fully-connected layer. Transformer encoder–decoder attention is this layer.

Figure 3: Layer Hopfield associates state patterns $\mathbf{R}$ with stored patterns $\mathbf{Y}$; output is a set $\mathbf{Z}$.
Figure 4: Layer HopfieldPooling summarizes a set via static, learnable query patterns, enabling multiple-instance learning.
Figure 5 (from paper): Layer HopfieldLayer queries a fixed, learnable stored set and can realize SVM-, $k$-nearest-neighbour-, and LVQ-like models.
Four control knobs. The energy analysis gives every layer four levers with no extra parameters: the number of update steps trades precision against compute; $\beta$ selects the fixed-point regime; the associative-space dimension sets capacity; and pattern normalization shapes the dynamics.

6.1 The update iteration in code

The heart of the implementation is a loop that applies Eq. (3) repeatedly until the state stops moving. The temperature $\beta$ enters as a per-head scaling factor on the query, and the loop terminates per head when successive states differ by less than a threshold.

# hflayers/functional.py — hopfield_core_forward (condensed)
while update_active_heads.any():
    if update_step > 0:
        # ξ_new = X · softmax(β Xᵀξ):  recompute the query from the
        # previous association weights before the next update
        q = torch.bmm(active_xi, active_k)

    q = q * scaling                          # scaling == β  (1/√d_k for attention)

    attn_output_weights = torch.bmm(q, k.transpose(1, 2))   # β · QKᵀ
    xi = nn.functional.softmax(attn_output_weights, dim=-1)  # the association p

    with torch.no_grad():                     # per-head early stop when ‖ξ_new − ξ_old‖ < eps
        update_active_heads = (update_step < update_steps_max) | (update_steps_max < 0)
        if xi_old is not None:
            update_active_heads &= (xi_old - xi_active).norm(p=2, dim=(2,3)).max(axis=0)[0] > update_steps_eps
        xi_old = xi_active
    update_step += 1

attn_output = torch.bmm(attn_output_weights, v)   # Z = p · V

The reprojection q = torch.bmm(active_xi, active_k) is the associative-memory recurrence $\boldsymbol{\xi}^{t+1} = \mathbf{X}\,\mathbf{p}^t$ — a plain attention layer never re-feeds its output as the next query. update_steps_max/eps expose the update-steps knob; a single step recovers ordinary attention. scaling is $\beta$ per head.

7. What the Layers and the Analysis Show

The evaluation has two halves mirroring the paper's two claims: an analytical half that reads BERT's heads via the equivalence, and an empirical half that drops the three layers into real tasks.

7.1 Reading BERT's attention heads

Each head is labelled by the size of the metastable state it averages over — the minimal $k$ of softmax weights reaching $0.90$ of the mass. Across depth: lower layers are class (I), near-global averaging; middle layers show large/small metastable states; last layers are predominantly class (III), medium metastable states.

8. Claims versus Implementation, and What It Left Open

The paper makes two kinds of claim — mathematical properties, and the identity with attention — and the released code lets each be checked against a running artifact.

Claim Status Evidence
Update rule equals scaled dot-product attention✓Derived (Eq. 10); a single update with scaling = 1/√d_k is exactly attention.
One-step retrieval of well-separated patterns✓Theorem with exponentially small error; the loop defaults to few steps and early-stops.
Exponential storage capacity in continuous states✓Proven $\propto c^{(d-1)/4}$; realized by storing ~300k patterns in the immune-repertoire task.
Multi-step retrieval sharpens the fixed point without new parameters◐Exposed as update_steps_max / eps; reported as a knob, but main results use few steps.
Last-layer heads are a promising target for improving transformers✗A hypothesis from the head analysis; not turned into an architecture change here.

Strengths

  • One identity unifies attention, pooling, associative memory, and lookup classifiers under one energy view.
  • Claims backed by proofs and a clean PyTorch implementation whose parameters map onto the theory.
  • The metastable-state analysis gives a measurable, interpretable summary of each BERT head.

Limitations

  • Gains are on set-structured and small-data tasks; no language-modelling improvement is demonstrated from the head insight.
  • Multi-step retrieval adds per-layer compute; its benefit over the single-step case is not quantified in the main experiments.
  • The capacity bound is for random patterns on a sphere; real learned patterns are correlated.
Why it still matters for agentic infrastructure. The associative-memory reading of attention is the conceptual root of later systems work: prefix and KV-cache reuse rests on retrieving a stored set of key–value patterns rather than recomputing it, and memory-augmented agents make that store explicit and external.
